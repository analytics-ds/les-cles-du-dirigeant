> **In brief:**
> 1. Best e-invoicing support combines an accounting firm piloting the project and an approved platform executing it technically. Accounting networks, consulting firms and software editors each offer support models suited to the situation.
> 2. The firm establishes diagnosis, arbitrates choices and verifies compliance, the approved platform formats the invoice and transmits it. These are two distinct skills, often confused.
> 3. Support follows six stages, from scoping diagnosis to platform choice, then configuration, real-world testing, team training and finally gradual switchover.
> 4. An approved platform never interprets regulation, it applies the rules it is given. Tax compliance is the firm's responsibility, not the platform's.
> 5. Skipping the diagnostic stage almost always leads to choosing a platform that does not connect to existing systems, creating double entry.

The most costly confusion over [mandatory e-invoicing](/en/blog/mandatory-e-invoicing-france/) concerns who does what. A business owner thinks they just need to pick a platform, then discovers nobody is piloting the project. Others think the approved platform decides their tax compliance and are surprised to learn it never does. Best support combines an accounting firm piloting the project and an approved platform executing it technically. Here is how roles and a successful transition really work.

## Clear division of skills

**The accounting firm.** It intervenes before tool choice and after it goes live. Its role is to frame the project, meaning establish diagnosis, arbitrate technically, verify tax compliance and train teams. A firm adds value because it knows the business file, its VAT regime, its accounting circuit and practices.

**The approved platform.** This is the mandatory tool that formats the invoice in structured form, transmits it to its destination and reports it to the authorities. It also handles archiving, recipient directory management and, depending on offerings, reminder tracking or bank reconciliation, useful for [managing cash flow in small businesses](/en/blog/managing-cash-flow-small-business/).

**What separates them.** The platform executes rules, it does not interpret them. It can correctly transmit an invoice that remains fiscally incorrect, because that is not its role to judge compliance. That is precisely why the firm side is not optional whenever there is complexity. An invoice can pass through an approved platform and remain non-compliant in substance.

## The six-stage flow

### Stage 1. Scope diagnosis, two to three weeks

The firm establishes the foundation, no platform ever intervenes here.

- Which invoices are subject based on customer type, B2B or B2C.
- When the requirement applies to the business.
- What formats customers expect and request.
- What route today's quotes, invoices and documents take to accounting.
- What tools are already in use, invoicing software, till, ERP, spreadsheet.

**Who does what.** The owner provides data. The firm establishes the requirement scope and real applicable deadline. This is the stage most often skipped to save time and costs most to recover. Many owners believe they are late on the emission requirement when they are only affected by receipt in the short term.

### Stage 2. Platform choice, one to two weeks

Choice is made on four criteria, in this strict order.

1. Connection to the existing setup. Does the platform plug into the current invoicing tool, or does it need manual re-entry.
2. Formats handled, Factur-X, UBL or CII, depending on what customers demand.
3. Invoice volume and resulting pricing model.
4. Route to transmit to accounting.

**Who does what.** The firm offers a documented pre-selection. The owner makes the final call. This order matters, because the firm knows the accounting circuit and knows in advance what will create friction.

### Stage 3. Configuration and connection, two to four weeks

All tools go live in parallel.

- Account and identifier setup.
- Registration in the recipient directory.
- Mandatory mention setup (VAT number, name, addresses).
- Flow setup between platform and accounting software.

**Who does what.** The platform configures its technical side. The firm sets up the accounting connection and verifies mandatory mentions are in place.

### Stage 4. Real-world testing, two weeks

You emit and receive real invoices with one or two volunteer customers, in production.

Rejections arrive here, usually a wrong VAT number, missing mention or format the customer will not accept. This is normal and expected.

**Who does what.** The owner identifies test customers. The firm analyses error messages and corrects. The platform supplies diagnostic information.

This stage is often rushed to save time. That is false economy, because rejections arrive anyway, but this time across all customers.

### Stage 5. Team training, one day maximum

People invoicing daily need to know the new procedure, read invoice status and especially know what to do with a rejection.

**Who does what.** The firm runs training, or the platform on its software part alone.

### Stage 6. Gradual transition and switchover, four to six weeks

You do not cut off the old circuit overnight. Run both systems in parallel long enough to verify nothing is lost, then switch gradually by flow.

**Who does what.** Owner and team pilot. Firm supplies regular checkpoints. Platform ensures its service.

## The point business owners discover too late

An approved platform never rules on your compliance. It transports and formats, it does not verify your VAT regime is correctly set up, your mandatory mentions are complete or your registration number is valid. An invoice can pass through a registered platform and remain non-compliant in substance.

That is why the [accounting firm](/en/blog/best-accounting-firm-france/) is not optional. It is the contact that rules on compliance and bears responsibility for the end result.

## Real project duration by complexity

For a single-site small business, expect two to four months from diagnosis to full switchover.

For a multi-site SME, the horizon extends to four to six months minimum, the key variable being how many different processes to harmonise.

For a mid-cap with an ERP to interface and international flows, the project extends to six to eight months, or longer if multiple countries must be handled in parallel.
