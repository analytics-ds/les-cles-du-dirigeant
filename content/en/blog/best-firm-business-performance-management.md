Many managers discover a margin drop or cash flow tension at year-end, several months after the facts. Performance management reverses this logic, by having the right indicators at the right time and an adviser capable of connecting them to decisions to make. Here is what a firm can provide in this area and how to choose yours. Performance management is part of the matters to check when [choosing your chartered accountant](/en/blog/how-to-choose-chartered-accountant/). ## What performance management covers

Performance management, also found under the English acronym EPM (Enterprise Performance Management), relies on three families of indicators:

- **activity and profitability indicators**, such as revenue, gross margin, profit and break-even point;
- **cash flow indicators**, such as cash position, customer and supplier payment terms and working capital;
- **forecast indicators**, such as budget forecast, cash flow plan and impact of an investment or hiring.

A dashboard has value only if it is read. The difference between accounting tracking and true management lies in the discussion accompanying figures, about the causes of a variance and the levers to correct it.

In Extenso states that "management support is the group's core purpose." Its approach is based on a simple principle, proactivity, that is, preventing before the event occurs.

Because the same firm handles taxation, payroll, legal matters, wealth and succession, an informed decision from the dashboard can be studied from every angle, for example the effect of hiring on cash flow and social charges, or that of an investment on taxation. ## How to choose your firm for performance management

1. **Ask for concrete examples.** A sample dashboard or cash flow plan shows better than a speech the level of management offered.
2. **Set monitoring frequency.** A growing company or under cash flow tension needs regular interim statements, a stable business can settle for less frequent monitoring.
3. **Identify your contact.** Management works through discussion with someone who knows your file and can connect figures to your projects.
4. **Check breadth of expertise.** Financing, taxation, payroll, succession, the more expertise a firm brings together, the more consistent decisions stemming from management are.
